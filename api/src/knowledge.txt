About: a student again, after 13 years
I'm a data guy. For almost fourteen years I moved data from where it was to where it was useful: SAP dashboards on the first retina iPads, central-bank data warehouses in Kuala Lumpur, Oracle-to-HANA migrations, Snowflake pipelines that saved real money.
For about three years I've lived in the AI world, playing with every model as it arrived. Two years before that, models weren't even that smart. I wanted to know how these things are actually built, so in 2024 I did something I hadn't done in thirteen years: I went back to the beginning. Some concepts I revisited from basics; many I learned from scratch.
In January 2025 I joined the M.Tech in Artificial Intelligence at IIT Jodhpur, and I finish in December 2026. I study between family time and a full-time job leading AI engineering at 3M. I've watched every generation of model grow up, from autocomplete to agents, and I've learned each step the way a student does.

TariffTrail
Oct 2026 · AWS Builder Center · Strands Decider
Finds a product's 10-digit US tariff code. Strands Decider 2B walks the official tariff tree one fork at a time, with "none of these" at every level, then one Amazon Bedrock call picks among real codes, so it cannot invent one. On 332 held-out 2026 CBP rulings: 36.7% exact and 0% invented codes, against 0.3% for Nova Pro asked directly, using 7.8× fewer tokens than a tool-calling agent. A human confirms every filing.

Silicon: Moore's law under every rewind
In 1965 Gordon Moore saw the number of components on a chip doubling every year; in 1975 he revised it to every two years. Every rewind moves this cursor to that year's silicon. Drag across the charts to visit any year since the Intel 4004.

My 14 years: the biggest chip went from 7.08 billion transistors (2012) to 336 billion (2026), about 47 times more. That is a doubling every 2.5 years, a little slower than Moore's two.
MOSFET scaling: process names fell from 22 nm to 2 nm, though the names no longer measure anything physical. The real gains came from new transistor shapes: FinFET in 2012, gate-all-around in 2025.
Clocks: top speed stopped climbing in the mid-2000s, when Dennard scaling ended. After that, more transistors meant more cores and bigger GPUs, the hardware my AI work runs on.
